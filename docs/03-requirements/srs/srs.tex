\section{Introduction}

Open Clinical Record is an extensible Electronic Medical Record (EMR) platform intended to provide a clear and maintainable foundation for common clinical workflows. The internship MVP deliberately focuses on three connected areas: the patient chart, medical records and reports, and appointment/visit management.

The system is not intended to be a complete hospital information system in the first release. The requirements therefore prioritize a coherent clinical workflow, reliable record handling, role-based access, and a design that can be expanded later without unnecessarily coupling future modules to the MVP.

\subsection{Purpose}

The purpose of this SRS is to define what the MVP shall do, the quality constraints it shall satisfy, and the rules that govern important clinical behavior. The SRS also provides a traceability basis between discovery findings, requirements, architecture, data design, and verification.

\subsection{Definitions}

\begin{longtable}{p{0.22\textwidth}p{0.68\textwidth}}
\toprule
Term & Definition \\
\midrule

Appointment & A scheduled interaction between a patient and a provider/service at a specified time. \\
Encounter & The clinical interaction in which assessment, examination, diagnosis, treatment, and related documentation are recorded. \\

\section{Product Vision}

The product should make the common clinical workflow understandable, connected, and reliable:

\begin{center}
Patient $\rightarrow$ Appointment $\rightarrow$ Visit/Encounter $\rightarrow$ Clinical Documentation $\rightarrow$ Longitudinal Patient Record
\end{center}

The patient chart is the main clinical context. Appointments and encounters should connect to the patient, and clinical documentation should remain linked to the appropriate encounter and patient throughout its lifecycle.

The first release should remain deliberately small while avoiding architectural choices that make later clinical expansion unnecessarily difficult.

\section{Scope}

\subsection{In Scope}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \item Patient registration, unique identification, search, profile, and clinical summary.
    \item Relevant patient history, allergies, medications, vitals, alerts, and current status.
    \item Deceased-patient status and associated lifecycle handling.
    \item Appointment creation, viewing, cancellation, rescheduling, history preservation, and check-in.
    \item Flexible walk-in handling.
    \item Basic appointment-to-encounter linkage.
    \item Encounter documentation including complaint, vitals, history, examination, diagnosis, treatment, prescription, and clinical notes.
    \item Medical report generation and clinical-document finalization.
    \item Controlled amendment of finalized clinical documentation without silently overwriting the original.
    \item Authentication, role-based authorization, audit logging, validation, and understandable error handling.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Deferred / Future Scope}
The following are outside the MVP unless later discovery explicitly changes the scope:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \item Laboratory information and laboratory-result workflows.
    \item Pharmacy and medication-dispensing workflows.
    \item Billing, insurance, and payment processing.
    \item Radiology and imaging workflows.
    \item Patient portal and patient-facing messaging.
    \item AI-assisted diagnosis, summarization, or clinical decision support.
    \item Dedicated mobile applications.
    \item Production-grade external interoperability integrations.
\end{itemize}

AI-assisted capabilities and mobile access were suggested during preliminary clinical feedback and are therefore retained as future opportunities rather than MVP requirements.

\section{Stakeholders and User Roles}

The MVP uses exactly three primary application roles. Permissions should be implemented through role-based authorization so that the system can refine individual permissions without creating unnecessary new user roles.

\begin{longtable}{p{0.22\textwidth}p{0.68\textwidth}}
\toprule
Role & Primary responsibilities \\
\midrule
Administrator & User/account administration, system configuration, access management, and authorized oversight/audit activities. \\
Clinical Staff & Patient registration and search support, appointment operations, check-in, vitals, and other permitted non-provider clinical workflow tasks. \\
Doctor/Clinician & Clinical assessment, history and examination documentation, diagnosis, treatment, prescriptions, clinical notes, medical reports, and authorized patient-status updates. \\
\bottomrule
\end{longtable}

\subsection{Role Boundaries}

Clinical actions shall not be granted merely because a user can access a patient record. In particular, diagnosis, treatment, clinical notes, medical reports, and clinical-status changes shall require appropriate clinical authorization. Appointment scheduling permissions may be assigned according to configured role permissions rather than being hard-coded to a separate receptionist role.

\section{Clinical Workflow}

The MVP follows an end-to-end workflow rather than treating patient, appointment, and medical-record features as unrelated CRUD modules.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
    \item An authorized user registers or searches for the patient.
    \item The patient's chart provides the clinical context, including demographics, status, relevant history, allergies, medications, alerts, and previous visits.
    \item An authorized user creates or views an appointment when the patient is scheduled.
    \item The patient is checked in, or a supported walk-in workflow is initiated when no appointment exists.
    \item A visit/encounter is created or accessed and linked to the patient and, when applicable, the appointment.
    \item Clinical staff may record permitted preliminary clinical information such as vitals.
    \item The Doctor/Clinician records the clinical assessment, examination, diagnosis, treatment, prescription, and clinical notes as applicable.
    \item A medical report or other clinical document may be generated and finalized.
    \item Once finalized, the document is protected from ordinary editing. An authorized correction is represented through an amendment that preserves the original.
    \item The resulting encounter and documents remain part of the patient's longitudinal record.
\end{enumerate}

Walk-in handling shall remain flexible because the appropriate path may depend on patient condition and urgency. The system shall not impose one universal walk-in process that assumes every walk-in should first become a normal scheduled appointment.

\section{Requirements Principles}

The requirements follow these principles:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \item Clinical workflow takes precedence over isolated CRUD operations.
    \item Patient and encounter linkage is a core integrity requirement.
    \item Historical clinical information should not be silently destroyed.
    \item Finalized clinical documents require stronger integrity controls than ordinary editable data.
    \item Access shall follow least privilege and role-based authorization.
    \item Requirements shall be traceable to research, discovery, validation, or explicit project decisions.
    \item Features observed in another EMR are not automatically requirements for Open Clinical Record.
    \item Future interoperability should be considered in the data and API boundaries without expanding the MVP into a full interoperability implementation.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Core Relationship Constraints}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \item Each patient shall have a unique identifier.
    \item Each encounter shall identify the patient to whom it belongs.
    \item An appointment may be linked to an encounter when the scheduled interaction results in a visit.
    \item A walk-in encounter may exist without a prior appointment.
    \item Clinical documents shall identify the patient and, where applicable, the encounter from which they originated.
    \item An amendment shall identify the finalized document it corrects and shall not replace the original content.
    \item Sensitive actions shall be attributable to an authenticated user through audit information.
\end{itemize}

\section{Constraints and Assumptions}

\subsection{Constraints}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \item The internship MVP has a deliberately limited scope and shall not attempt to implement every EMR module.
    \item Development and deployment choices should favor maintainable and accessible technologies suitable for the internship environment.
    \item The system must treat patient information as sensitive data and apply appropriate access controls.
    \item External service or network outages cannot be assumed to be absent; failure handling is therefore part of the requirements.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Assumptions}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \item A deployment environment will provide persistent storage for application data.
    \item Authorized users will have accounts associated with one of the three MVP roles.
    \item Exact clinical terminology, mandatory fields, and operational policies may require additional validation with clinical stakeholders.
    \item The MVP will not independently provide clinical decision-making; it records and presents information entered by authorized users.
\end{itemize}

\section{Clinical Validation Evidence}

A preliminary survey response was received from a Doctor/Clinician. The response supports the inclusion of patient demographics, conditions, previous diagnoses, vitals, allergies, medications, previous visits, configurable appointment permissions, preservation of cancelled and rescheduled appointments, core encounter information, clinician-controlled diagnosis/treatment/notes/reports/status changes, amendment rather than silent replacement of finalized reports, clinical alerts, and deceased-patient handling.

The respondent also identified network reliability as a practical concern. AI-assisted capabilities and mobile access were suggested as future opportunities rather than immediate MVP requirements.

This evidence is preliminary and is not treated as statistical validation. Additional clinician or stakeholder review should be used to confirm workflow details and resolve remaining policy decisions before a final production baseline.

\section{Open Decisions for Review}

The following items should be confirmed during mentor and stakeholder review rather than hidden as assumptions:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \item Exact required demographic and identification fields for patient registration.
    \item Exact appointment status values and rescheduling-history representation.
    \item Exact policy for future appointments when a patient is marked deceased, including whether they are automatically cancelled or flagged for authorized review.
    \item Exact clinical-document amendment permissions and required amendment reason fields.
    \item Exact session-timeout value and deployment-specific performance environment.
    \item Backup frequency, retention, and recovery-time expectations for the eventual deployment.
    \item Final terminology and clinical-field validation with additional clinical stakeholders.
\end{itemize}

These decisions do not prevent architecture work; they should be represented as explicit configurable or reviewable decisions where appropriate.

\section{Future Expansion Considerations}

The MVP should leave clean boundaries for future modules without implementing them prematurely. Likely expansion areas include laboratory, pharmacy, billing, radiology, patient-facing access, mobile access, AI-assisted functionality, and standards-based interoperability.

Future functionality should build on the existing patient, appointment, encounter, clinical-document, authorization, and audit concepts rather than introducing parallel representations of the same clinical facts.
