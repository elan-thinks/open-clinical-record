\section{Introduction}
\subsection{Purpose}
This Software Requirements Specification defines the reduced and prioritized MVP for Open Clinical Record (OCR). The scope has been revised following mentor feedback to fit the remaining one-month internship period.

The MVP is focused on three core business areas: \textbf{Patient Management}, \textbf{Patient Chart}, and \textbf{Appointment Management}. The objective is to deliver these workflows as a coherent, working system before considering additional functionality.

\subsection{Scope of Product}
Open Clinical Record is a focused outpatient patient-management and appointment system with a basic electronic patient chart. The patient is the central record around which the three MVP areas are connected.

\begin{center}\textbf{Patient Management $\rightarrow$ Patient Chart $\rightarrow$ Appointment Management $\rightarrow$ Check-in/Visit History}\end{center}

The MVP does not attempt to implement a complete hospital EMR. Full clinical encounter documentation, diagnosis, treatment, prescriptions, advanced medical reports, laboratory, pharmacy, billing, radiology, patient portal, advanced analytics, and standards-based interoperability are deferred.

\section{General Description}
\subsection{Product Perspective}
The system is designed around a simple clinic workflow. Staff first identify or register the patient, then access the patient chart, manage an appointment, and record attendance/check-in information. The resulting appointment and visit history remains associated with the patient.

The system should be modular enough to support future expansion, but future capabilities must not delay completion of the three approved MVP areas.

\subsection{MVP Modules}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{Patient Management}: patient registration, unique identifier, search, profile, demographic/contact information, and basic status.
\item \textbf{Patient Chart}: patient context, allergies, relevant medication/history information, alerts where required, and appointment/visit history.
\item \textbf{Appointment Management}: appointment creation, viewing, rescheduling, cancellation, status, check-in, and basic walk-in support.
\end{enumerate}

Authentication, authorization, validation, error handling, and basic audit logging are cross-cutting support concerns. They are not additional business modules.

\subsection{User Characteristics}
The MVP has exactly three application roles.

\begin{longtable}{P{0.24\textwidth}P{0.28\textwidth}P{0.36\textwidth}}
\toprule Role & Responsibility & MVP capabilities \\\midrule
\textbf{Receptionist / Front Desk} & Patient registration and scheduling & Register/search patients; maintain permitted demographics; create, view, reschedule and cancel appointments; check in patients; support basic walk-ins. \\
\textbf{Nurse / Clinical Staff} & Clinical support & Search/view patients and charts; view appointments; support check-in/visit workflow; maintain permitted chart information according to the approved permission matrix. \\
\textbf{Clinician / Doctor} & Clinical oversight and patient care context & Search/view patient charts; review patient history and appointments; perform authorized clinical/chart updates required by the MVP. \\
\bottomrule
\end{longtable}

No System Administrator or other application role is included in the MVP.

\subsection{Constraints}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\item The MVP must remain small enough to implement and test within the remaining one-month internship period.
\item Only three business modules are committed: Patient Management, Patient Chart, and Appointment Management.
\item Exactly three application roles are used: Receptionist/Front Desk, Nurse/Clinical Staff, and Clinician/Doctor.
\item International healthcare standards and FHIR integration are not required for the internship MVP.
\item Additional functionality may be added only after the core MVP is complete and only if time remains.
\item Patient information must be protected from unauthorized access.
\end{itemize}

\section{Specific Requirements}
\subsection{External Interface Requirements}
\subsubsection{User Interface}
The system shall provide clear screens for authentication, patient search, patient registration, patient profile/chart, appointment management, appointment details, check-in, and basic visit history.

The interface shall show the current patient context clearly and shall not present controls for operations the current user is not authorized to perform.

\subsubsection{Software Interface}
The React frontend shall communicate with the ASP.NET Core backend through the REST API defined by the architecture. The backend shall enforce validation and authorization independently of the frontend.

\subsubsection{Communication}
Client/server communication shall use secure transport in the deployed application. Communication or save failures shall be reported clearly and shall not falsely indicate successful persistence.

\subsubsection{Patient Management Requirements}

\req{FR-AP-009}{Visit linkage}{Check-in/visit information shall remain linked to the correct patient and, when applicable, the originating appointment.}

\subsubsection{Authentication, Authorization, Validation and Audit}
\begin{longtable}{P{0.14\textwidth}P{0.20\textwidth}P{0.56\textwidth}}

\req{FR-SEC-001}{Authenticate user}{The system shall authenticate users before granting access to protected functions or patient information.}
\req{FR-SEC-002}{Enforce three roles}{The system shall enforce permissions for exactly three MVP roles: Receptionist/Front Desk, Nurse/Clinical Staff, and Clinician/Doctor.}
\req{FR-SEC-003}{Restrict access}{The system shall deny operations for which the authenticated user lacks permission.}
\req{FR-SEC-004}{Audit important actions}{The system shall record important patient and appointment actions with actor and timestamp information where required.}

\subsection{Business Rules}
\begin{longtable}{P{0.14\textwidth}P{0.76\textwidth}}
\toprule ID & Business rule \\\midrule
BR-001 & Each patient has one unique patient identifier within the system. \\
BR-002 & Patient chart information must belong to the correct patient. \\
BR-003 & Cancelled appointments remain in history rather than being silently deleted. \\
BR-004 & Rescheduling shall not erase the fact that an earlier appointment state existed. \\
BR-005 & A walk-in may create a visit without a prior appointment. \\

\subsection{Non-Functional Requirements}
The existing detailed NFR document remains supporting material, but the internship MVP prioritizes the following qualities:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{Usability:} common patient and appointment tasks should be straightforward for clinic staff.
\item \textbf{Performance:} ordinary patient search and appointment operations should respond promptly under expected internship-scale usage.
\item \textbf{Security:} authenticated users must only access permitted patient information and operations.
\item \textbf{Data integrity:} patient and appointment records must not be silently corrupted or lost.
\item \textbf{Reliability:} failed operations must produce safe, understandable outcomes.
\item \textbf{Maintainability:} the implementation should keep the three modules separated enough for future extension.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Logical Database Requirements}
The database shall support the three MVP modules without introducing unnecessary enterprise complexity.

\textbf{Patient Management:} Patient.

\textbf{Patient Chart:} Allergy, Medication History, Patient Alert, and patient-linked appointment/visit history.

\textbf{Appointment Management:} Appointment, Appointment History/Event where required, Visit.

\textbf{Cross-cutting:} User, Audit Event where implemented.

The central relationships are Patient $1$--$M$ Appointment, Patient $1$--$M$ Visit, and Appointment $0..1$--$1$ Visit where a visit originated from an appointment. A walk-in visit shall be allowed without an appointment reference.

The final ERD should use the simplest normalized model that supports the approved MVP and can be implemented within the remaining project period.

\subsection{Deferred Functionality}
The following are explicitly outside the committed MVP and shall not be used to expand the implementation schedule:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\item Full encounter documentation
\item Diagnosis and treatment documentation
\item Prescription management
\item Advanced medical report/document lifecycle
\item Document amendments/versioning
\item Laboratory, pharmacy, billing, insurance, and radiology modules
\item Patient portal/mobile application
\item SMS and external integrations
\item FHIR or other international standards implementation
\item Advanced analytics and enterprise scheduling
\item AI clinical decision support
\end{itemize}

These may be considered as future work only after the MVP is functional and tested.

\subsection{Acceptance Criteria}
The MVP is considered ready for demonstration when the following can be completed successfully:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
\item A receptionist can register a patient and receive a unique patient ID.
\item An authorized user can search for and open that patient.
\item The patient's chart displays the stored patient information and relevant history.
\item Authorized staff can record and view supported chart information.
\item A receptionist can create an appointment for the patient.
\item Authorized staff can view the appointment and update its status/check in the patient.
\item An appointment can be rescheduled or cancelled without silently deleting its history.
\item A basic walk-in can be recorded without inventing a previous appointment.
\item Role restrictions prevent unauthorized operations.
\item Invalid input and failed operations are handled without creating inconsistent records.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Scope Change Control}
Any new feature proposed during implementation should first be classified as either MVP or Future. A Future feature shall not displace unfinished core functionality unless the mentor explicitly approves the scope change.
