\section{Introduction}
\subsection{Purpose of the Requirements Document}
This Software Requirements Specification defines the functional behavior, quality requirements, interfaces, constraints, data requirements, business rules, and acceptance expectations for the Open Clinical Record (OCR) MVP. Its purpose is to establish a clear and testable baseline before architecture, detailed data modeling, and implementation are finalized.

The document translates the project's research and clinical workflow discovery into explicit requirements. It establishes traceability from workflow and evidence through requirements and, later, design and verification. A requirement appearing here does not by itself mean that it has already been implemented or clinically validated.

\subsection{Scope of Product}
Open Clinical Record is an extensible Electronic Medical Record platform for a focused set of common outpatient workflows. The MVP centers on: (1) a longitudinal patient chart; (2) appointments, visits, check-in, and basic walk-in handling; and (3) clinical encounters, medical records, and reports with appropriate document integrity.

Laboratory, pharmacy, billing, insurance, radiology, patient portal, advanced analytics, autonomous AI clinical decision support, and dedicated mobile applications are outside the MVP unless later approved through requirements change control.

\section{General Description}
\subsection{Product Perspective}
Open Clinical Record is conceived as an extensible EMR platform rather than unrelated CRUD screens. The patient chart is the principal clinical context. Appointments and encounters are linked to the patient, and clinical documentation is linked to the encounter and patient so that the longitudinal record remains coherent.

\begin{center}\textbf{Patient Registration/Search $\rightarrow$ Chart $\rightarrow$ Appointment/Visit $\rightarrow$ Check-in $\rightarrow$ Encounter $\rightarrow$ Clinical Documentation $\rightarrow$ Longitudinal Record}\end{center}

\subsection{Product Functions}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
\item Patient registration and unique identification.
\item Patient search and chart access.
\item Clinical summary, allergies, medications, alerts, and patient status.
\item Appointment creation, viewing, rescheduling, cancellation, history, and check-in.
\item Flexible walk-in handling.
\item Patient-to-visit-to-encounter linkage.
\item Clinical encounter documentation: complaint, vitals, history, examination, diagnosis, treatment, prescription, and notes.
\item Medical report generation and document lifecycle management.
\item Finalization and controlled amendment of clinical documents.
\item Authentication, role-based authorization, auditability, and privacy protection.
\item Input validation and safe error handling.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{User Characteristics}
The MVP has exactly three primary application roles. These are application roles, not a claim that every deployment uses identical job titles.

\begin{longtable}{P{0.23\textwidth}P{0.28\textwidth}P{0.39\textwidth}}
\toprule Role & Typical responsibility & Primary system capabilities \\\midrule
\textbf{Receptionist / Front Desk} & Patient-facing administrative workflow & Register/search patients; manage permitted demographic information; create, view, reschedule, and cancel appointments; perform check-in; initiate supported walk-in intake. No diagnosis, treatment, prescribing, or clinical-report authoring. \\
\textbf{Nurse / Clinical Staff} & Clinical workflow support and nursing activities & Search patients; support appointment/check-in workflow; record permitted vitals/observations; support encounters within assigned permissions. Appointment creation by this role remains subject to the approved permission matrix. \\
\textbf{Clinician / Doctor} & Clinical assessment and clinical decision documentation & Review chart; assess patient; record history/examination, diagnosis, treatment, prescription where applicable; create/finalize permitted clinical notes/reports; perform authorized patient-status changes and amendments. \\
\bottomrule
\end{longtable}

Permissions shall be enforced at operation and data level. The final permission matrix requires mentor/clinical stakeholder confirmation; unresolved policy questions are not implementation authority.

\subsection{General Constraints}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\item The MVP remains within patient chart, appointment/visit, and medical record/report scope.
\item Sensitive patient and clinical information shall be protected from unauthorized access.
\item Clinically significant history shall not be silently destroyed.
\item Finalized clinical documents require stronger integrity protection than ordinary draft content.
\item Application boundaries should support future interoperability without requiring MVP FHIR integration.
\item The MVP shall not make autonomous clinical decisions.
\item Walk-in handling remains flexible because urgency and patient condition may affect the appropriate path.
\end{itemize}

\section{Specific Requirements}
\subsection{External Interface Requirements}
\subsubsection{User Interface Requirements}
The application shall provide a consistent interface for the three approved roles. The UI shall make patient identity/current context clear, distinguish editable drafts from finalized documents, and present appointment status explicitly. It shall include conceptual views for authentication, patient search/registration, chart, appointments, check-in/walk-in, encounter workspace, clinical documentation, amendment history, and error/access-denied states.

The UI shall not expose controls for actions the current user is not permitted to perform. The service/application layer shall independently enforce authorization.

\subsubsection{Software Interfaces}
The system shall use explicit boundaries so UI code does not directly manipulate clinical persistence structures. Conceptual boundaries shall distinguish authentication/authorization, patient management, scheduling, encounter/documentation, persistence, and audit concerns. If an API layer is used, endpoints shall represent domain operations rather than unrestricted database CRUD.

\subsubsection{Communication Interfaces}
Client/server communication shall use secure transport appropriate to deployment. Protected information shall not be transmitted through unprotected channels in production. Communication failures shall be reported as failures unless persistence is confirmed.

\subsection{Non-Functional Requirements}
The detailed NFR catalogue is maintained in \texttt{docs/03-requirements/non-functional-requirements.md}. The SRS treats that catalogue as the canonical detailed NFR source; this section establishes the quality categories and does not intentionally duplicate every NFR entry.

Required categories are: security/privacy; data integrity and clinical record safety; performance; reliability/failure handling; usability/accessibility; maintainability/extensibility; interoperability/FHIR readiness; auditability; and backup/recovery.

\subsection{Logical Database Requirements}
The logical data model shall represent Patient, Allergy, Medication, Appointment, Visit, Encounter, Vital Sign, Clinical Note, Diagnosis, Treatment, Prescription, Clinical Document, Document Amendment, User, and Audit Event. Exact field types, indexes, normalization, and physical schema belong to the data-design stage.

Core relationships shall preserve patient-to-appointment, patient-to-visit, patient-to-encounter, appointment-to-visit, visit-to-encounter, patient/encounter-to-document, document-to-amendment, and actor-to-audit-event associations as appropriate. Current state and historical state shall be distinguishable where history preservation is required.

\subsection{Design Constraints}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
\item The MVP shall use exactly three approved application roles: Clinician/Doctor, Nurse/Clinical Staff, and Receptionist/Front Desk.
\item The MVP shall not include autonomous AI diagnostic or treatment functionality.
\item Finalized clinical documents shall not be ordinary mutable comments.
\item Patient and encounter relationships shall be first-class integrity constraints.
\item Future interoperability shall be supported without requiring production FHIR integration in the MVP.
\item The system shall remain modular enough for later clinical modules.
\item Security and privacy controls are requirements, not optional enhancements.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Appendix B --- Data-Flow and Workflow Models}
\begin{center}\textbf{User $\rightarrow$ Authentication/Authorization $\rightarrow$ Application Services $\rightarrow$ Clinical Domain Operations $\rightarrow$ Persistent Data + Audit}\end{center}

The clinical workflow is: register/identify patient; open chart; create/retrieve appointment or initiate walk-in; check in; create/access encounter; record permitted vitals/preliminary information; clinician assesses and records findings; clinician records diagnosis/treatment/prescription/notes as applicable; generate/finalize document; preserve encounter/document in longitudinal record; amend finalized documents through a traceable correction path when authorized.

\subsection{Appendix C --- Detailed Domain/Object Model}
Patient is the central clinical context with explicit links to Appointment, Visit, and Encounter. Encounter is the principal clinical context for assessment and care documentation. Clinical documents link to patient and relevant encounter.

Lifecycle concepts: Appointment = scheduled $\rightarrow$ checked-in/attended, cancelled, no-show, or rescheduled as applicable; Visit = appointment or walk-in $\rightarrow$ checked-in/active $\rightarrow$ completed; Encounter = active $\rightarrow$ completed/closed; Clinical document = draft/in-progress $\rightarrow$ finalized/signed $\rightarrow$ amended when authorized; Patient = active/current status with deceased status affecting future scheduling.
