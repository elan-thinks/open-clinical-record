\section{Introduction}

Open Clinical Record is an extensible Electronic Medical Record (EMR) platform. The internship MVP focuses on three connected workflows: managing the patient chart, documenting medical records and reports, and managing appointments through the basic visit/consultation flow.

The system is being designed as a foundation for future clinical modules rather than as a complete hospital information system.

\section{Product Vision}

The product should make the common clinical workflow understandable and reliable:

\begin{center}
Patient $\rightarrow$ Appointment $\rightarrow$ Visit/Encounter $\rightarrow$ Clinical Documentation $\rightarrow$ Longitudinal Patient Record
\end{center}

The first release should stay deliberately small while avoiding decisions that make later expansion unnecessarily difficult.

\section{Scope}

\subsection{In Scope}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \item Patient registration, search, profile, and clinical summary.
    \item Relevant patient history, allergies, alerts, medications, and status.
    \item Deceased status and associated lifecycle handling.
    \item Appointment creation and management, including cancellation and rescheduling history.
    \item Basic check-in and visit/encounter linkage.
    \item Clinical notes, diagnoses, examination findings, treatment, and prescriptions where applicable to the MVP.
    \item Medical report generation and finalized clinical documentation.
    \item Controlled amendment of finalized clinical documentation without silently overwriting the original.
    \item Authentication, authorization, validation, audit logging, and error handling.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Deferred / Future}
Laboratory, pharmacy, billing, radiology, insurance, patient portal, messaging integrations, AI-assisted features, mobile access, and production-grade external interoperability are candidates for later phases unless discovery changes the scope.

\section{Requirements Principles}

Requirements must be supported by research, clinical workflow understanding, or explicit stakeholder decisions. A feature observed in another EMR is not automatically a requirement for this product.

\section{Business Rules}
\begin{longtable}{p{0.18\textwidth}p{0.72\textwidth}}
\toprule
ID & Rule \\
\midrule
BR-001 & Cancelled appointments shall remain part of appointment history and shall be visibly marked as cancelled. \\
BR-002 & Rescheduled appointments shall retain sufficient history to distinguish the original scheduling event from the updated appointment. \\
BR-003 & A finalized medical report shall not be silently overwritten. Corrections shall be represented as amendments/corrections with traceable history. \\
BR-004 & Diagnosis, treatment, clinical notes, medical reports, and patient clinical-status changes shall be protected by role-based authorization. \\
BR-005 & Patient alerts shall remain visible where clinically relevant and shall include recorded allergies and other configured high-priority risks. \\
BR-006 & A deceased patient shall not receive inappropriate new future appointments; affected future appointments shall be handled according to the configured policy. \\
BR-007 & Walk-in handling shall remain flexible because the appropriate workflow may depend on patient condition and urgency. \\
BR-008 & Clinical information shall remain linked to the correct patient and encounter throughout its lifecycle. \\
\bottomrule
\end{longtable}

\section{Clinical Validation Note}
A preliminary survey response was received from a Doctor/Clinician. The response supports the importance of patient demographics, conditions, previous diagnoses, vitals, allergies, medications, visit history, role separation for clinical actions, preservation of cancelled/rescheduled appointments, amendment rather than silent replacement of finalized reports, clinical alerts, and deceased-patient handling. The respondent also identified network reliability as a practical concern and suggested AI-assisted capabilities and mobile access as future opportunities.

This is preliminary evidence rather than statistical validation. The AI and mobile suggestions are therefore recorded as future enhancements rather than MVP requirements.

\section{Non-Functional Requirements}

The following quality requirements apply to the MVP. They are written so that they can be objectively checked where practical. Exact targets may be adjusted after deployment constraints and mentor review are confirmed.

\subsection{Security and Privacy}
\begin{longtable}{p{0.14\textwidth}p{0.28\textwidth}p{0.50\textwidth}}
\toprule
ID & Requirement & Description \\
\midrule
NFR-SEC-001 & Authentication & All protected clinical functions shall require successful user authentication. \\
NFR-SEC-002 & Authorization & Every protected clinical operation shall be checked against the authenticated user's role/permissions before execution. \\
NFR-SEC-003 & Least privilege & Users shall receive only the permissions required for their assigned responsibilities; clinical permissions shall not be granted by default to non-clinical roles. \\
NFR-SEC-004 & Session protection & The system shall terminate or invalidate inactive authenticated sessions according to a configured timeout policy. \\
NFR-SEC-005 & Password protection & User passwords shall never be stored in plaintext and shall be stored using an accepted one-way password-hashing mechanism. \\
NFR-SEC-006 & Privacy & Patient information shall only be exposed through authorized application functions and shall not be included in unnecessary error messages or logs. \\
\bottomrule
\end{longtable}

\section{Open Questions}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \item Which patient fields are mandatory in the target clinical setting?
    \item Which clinical documentation must be structured versus free text?
    \item What is the exact signing/finalization workflow for medical reports?
    \item Who may mark a patient as deceased, and what information must be captured?
    \item Should future appointments be automatically cancelled or merely blocked/flagged after deceased status?
    \item Which staff roles may create, modify, or cancel appointments in the target setting?
    \item Which terminology and interoperability concepts should be represented in the MVP?
    \item What network-failure behavior is required in the target deployment environment?
    \item What operating hours and maintenance window should be used when measuring availability?
    \item What database backup frequency and retention period are appropriate for the target deployment?
\end{itemize}

\section{Traceability}
The final SRS will link requirements to design decisions, implementation issues, tests, and release evidence. This is intended to make it possible to answer: \emph{why does this feature exist, where is it implemented, and how was it verified?}
