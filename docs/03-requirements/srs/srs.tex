\section{Introduction}
\subsection{Purpose of the Requirements Document}
This Software Requirements Specification defines the functional behavior, quality requirements, interfaces, constraints, data requirements, business rules, and acceptance expectations for the Open Clinical Record (OCR) MVP. Its purpose is to establish a clear and testable baseline before architecture, detailed data modeling, and implementation are finalized.

The document translates the project's research and clinical workflow discovery into explicit requirements. It establishes traceability from workflow and evidence through requirements and, later, design and verification. A requirement appearing here does not by itself mean that it has already been implemented or clinically validated.

\subsection{Scope of Product}
Open Clinical Record is an extensible Electronic Medical Record platform for a focused set of common outpatient workflows. The MVP is organized into exactly three business modules: (1) Medical Chart; (2) Appointments; and (3) Medical Records. These modules are connected through the patient and care workflow. Authentication, authorization, and audit are cross-cutting concerns and are not treated as a fourth business module.

The \textbf{Medical Chart} provides longitudinal patient context, including identity, demographics, status, allergies, medication history, alerts, and relevant historical clinical information. The \textbf{Appointments} module covers planned care, visits, check-in, scheduling, and supported walk-ins. The \textbf{Medical Records} module covers encounters, clinical assessment, treatment documentation, prescriptions, clinical notes, medical reports, document finalization, and amendments.

Laboratory, pharmacy, billing, insurance, radiology, patient portal, advanced analytics, autonomous AI clinical decision support, and dedicated mobile applications are outside the MVP unless later approved through requirements change control.

\section{General Description}
\subsection{Product Perspective}
Open Clinical Record is conceived as an extensible EMR platform rather than unrelated CRUD screens. The patient is the central clinical subject. The Medical Chart provides longitudinal context; Appointments represent planned/operational care; and Medical Records represent documentation of care actually delivered.

\begin{center}\textbf{Patient $\rightarrow$ Medical Chart $\rightarrow$ Appointment or Walk-in $\rightarrow$ Visit $\rightarrow$ Encounter $\rightarrow$ Medical Record/Report $\rightarrow$ Longitudinal Chart}\end{center}

An appointment, visit, and encounter are distinct concepts. A walk-in may create a Visit and Encounter without requiring a fabricated Appointment. A clinical document belongs to the appropriate patient and, where applicable, encounter. Historical states shall remain distinguishable when required for integrity or auditability.

\subsection{Product Functions}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
\item Medical Chart: patient registration, unique identification, search, profile, clinical summary, allergies, medication history, alerts, and patient status.
\item Appointments: appointment creation, viewing, rescheduling, cancellation, history, check-in, visits, and supported walk-ins.
\item Medical Records: encounters, chief complaint, vitals, history, examination, diagnosis, treatment/care plan, prescriptions, clinical notes, medical reports, finalization, and amendments.
\item Cross-cutting security: authentication, exactly three application roles, authorization, auditability, and privacy protection.
\item Input validation, transaction safety, and understandable error handling.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{User Characteristics}
The MVP has exactly three primary application roles. These are application roles, not a claim that every deployment uses identical job titles.

\begin{longtable}{P{0.23\textwidth}P{0.28\textwidth}P{0.39\textwidth}}
\toprule Role & Typical responsibility & Primary system capabilities \\\midrule
\textbf{Receptionist / Front Desk} & Patient-facing administrative workflow & Register/search patients; manage permitted demographic information; create, view, reschedule, and cancel appointments; perform check-in; initiate supported walk-in intake. No diagnosis, treatment, prescribing, or clinical-report authoring. \\
\textbf{Nurse / Clinical Staff} & Clinical workflow support and nursing activities & Search patients; support appointment/check-in workflow; record permitted vitals/observations; support encounters within assigned permissions. Additional administrative permissions remain subject to the approved matrix. \\
\textbf{Clinician / Doctor} & Clinical assessment and clinical decision documentation & Review chart; assess patient; record history/examination, diagnosis, treatment, prescription where applicable; create/finalize permitted clinical notes/reports; perform authorized patient-status changes and amendments. \\
\bottomrule
\end{longtable}

Permissions shall be enforced at operation and data level. The final permission matrix requires mentor/clinical stakeholder confirmation; unresolved policy questions are not implementation authority.

\subsection{General Constraints}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\item The MVP remains within the three business modules defined in Section 1.2.
\item Sensitive patient and clinical information shall be protected from unauthorized access.
\item Clinically significant history shall not be silently destroyed.
\item Finalized clinical documents require stronger integrity protection than ordinary draft content.
\item Application boundaries should support future interoperability without requiring MVP FHIR integration.
\item The MVP shall not make autonomous clinical decisions.
\item Walk-in handling remains flexible because urgency and patient condition may affect the appropriate path.
\end{itemize}

\section{Specific Requirements}
\subsection{External Interface Requirements}
\subsubsection{User Interface Requirements}
The application shall provide a consistent interface for the three approved roles. The UI shall make patient identity/current context clear, distinguish editable drafts from finalized documents, and present appointment status explicitly. It shall include conceptual views for authentication, patient search/registration, chart, appointments, check-in/walk-in, encounter workspace, clinical documentation, amendment history, and error/access-denied states.

The UI shall not expose controls for actions the current user is not permitted to perform. The service/application layer shall independently enforce authorization.

\subsubsection{Software Interfaces}
The system shall use explicit boundaries so UI code does not directly manipulate clinical persistence structures. Conceptual boundaries shall distinguish authentication/authorization, patient management, scheduling, encounter/documentation, persistence, and audit concerns. If an API layer is used, endpoints shall represent domain operations rather than unrestricted database CRUD.

\subsubsection{Communication Interfaces}
Client/server communication shall use secure transport appropriate to deployment. Protected information shall not be transmitted through unprotected channels in production. Communication failures shall be reported as failures unless persistence is confirmed.

\subsection{Non-Functional Requirements}
The detailed NFR catalogue is maintained in \texttt{docs/03-requirements/non-functional-requirements.md}. The SRS treats that catalogue as the canonical detailed NFR source; this section establishes the quality categories and does not intentionally duplicate every NFR entry.

Required categories are: security/privacy; data integrity and clinical record safety; performance; reliability/failure handling; usability/accessibility; maintainability/extensibility; interoperability/FHIR readiness; auditability; and backup/recovery.

\subsection{Logical Database Requirements}
The logical data model shall represent the entities needed to support the three business modules and their cross-cutting controls.

\textbf{Medical Chart entities:} Patient, Allergy, Medication History, Patient Alert.

\textbf{Appointments entities:} Appointment, Appointment History/Event, Visit.

\textbf{Medical Records entities:} Encounter, Vital Sign, Clinical Note, Diagnosis, Treatment/Care Plan, Prescription, Clinical Document/Medical Report, Document Amendment.

\textbf{Cross-cutting entities:} User, Audit Event.

Patient status, including deceased status and date of death, shall be represented explicitly in the patient model. Appointment history shall be represented through a dedicated history/event structure or an equivalent design that preserves significant state transitions. The final ERD shall choose the simplest normalized representation that satisfies these requirements.

Core relationships shall preserve patient-to-appointment, patient-to-visit, patient-to-encounter, appointment-to-visit, visit-to-encounter, patient/encounter-to-document, document-to-amendment, and actor-to-audit-event associations as appropriate. Medication history shall be associated with the patient and may reference a prescription when clinically appropriate without becoming the same entity. Current state and historical state shall be distinguishable where history preservation is required.

\subsection{Design Constraints}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
\item The MVP shall use exactly three approved application roles: Clinician/Doctor, Nurse/Clinical Staff, and Receptionist/Front Desk.
\item The MVP shall contain exactly three business modules: Medical Chart, Appointments, and Medical Records. Authentication, authorization, and audit remain cross-cutting concerns.
\item The MVP shall not include autonomous AI diagnostic or treatment functionality.
\item Finalized clinical documents shall not be ordinary mutable comments.
\item Patient, appointment, visit, and encounter relationships shall be first-class integrity constraints.
\item Future interoperability shall be supported without requiring production FHIR integration in the MVP.
\item The system shall remain modular enough for later clinical modules.
\item Security and privacy controls are requirements, not optional enhancements.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Appendix B --- Data-Flow and Workflow Models}
\begin{center}\textbf{User $\rightarrow$ Authentication/Authorization $\rightarrow$ Application Services $\rightarrow$ Clinical Domain Operations $\rightarrow$ Persistent Data + Audit}\end{center}

The clinical workflow is: register/identify patient; open Medical Chart; create/retrieve Appointment or initiate walk-in; create Visit/check in; create/access Encounter; record permitted vitals/preliminary information; clinician assesses and records findings; clinician records diagnosis/treatment/prescription/notes as applicable; generate/finalize Medical Record document/report; preserve encounter/document in longitudinal chart; amend finalized documents through a traceable correction path when authorized.

\subsection{Appendix C --- Detailed Domain/Object Model}
Patient is the central clinical subject with explicit links to Appointment, Visit, and Encounter. The Medical Chart is the longitudinal context, Appointments represent planned/operational care, and Medical Records represent documentation of care delivered. Encounter is the principal clinical context for assessment and care documentation. Clinical documents link to patient and relevant encounter.

Lifecycle concepts: Appointment = scheduled $\rightarrow$ checked-in/attended, cancelled, no-show, or rescheduled as applicable; Appointment History/Event preserves significant transitions; Visit = appointment or walk-in $\rightarrow$ checked-in/active $\rightarrow$ completed; Encounter = active $\rightarrow$ completed/closed; Clinical document = draft/in-progress $\rightarrow$ finalized/signed $\rightarrow$ amended when authorized; Patient = active/current status with deceased status affecting future scheduling.
