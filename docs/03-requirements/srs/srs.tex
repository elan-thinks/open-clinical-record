\section{Introduction}

Open Clinical Record is an extensible Electronic Medical Record (EMR) platform. The internship MVP focuses on three connected workflows: managing the patient chart, documenting medical records and reports, and managing appointments through the basic visit/consultation flow.

The system is being designed as a foundation for future clinical modules rather than as a complete hospital information system.

\section{Product Vision}

The product should make the common clinical workflow understandable and reliable:

\begin{center}
Patient \(\rightarrow\) Appointment \(\rightarrow\) Visit/Encounter \(\rightarrow\) Clinical Documentation \(\rightarrow\) Longitudinal Patient Record
\end{center}

The first release should stay deliberately small while avoiding decisions that make later expansion unnecessarily difficult.

\section{Scope}

\subsection{In Scope}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \item Patient registration, search, profile, and clinical summary.
    \item Relevant patient history, allergies, alerts, and status.
    \item Deceased status and associated lifecycle handling where required by validated clinical requirements.
    \item Appointment creation and management.
    \item Basic check-in and visit/encounter linkage.
    \item Clinical notes, diagnoses, and agreed treatment information.
    \item Medical report generation and finalized clinical documentation.
    \item Authentication, authorization, validation, audit logging, and error handling.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Deferred / Future}
Laboratory, pharmacy, billing, radiology, insurance, patient portal, messaging integrations, and production-grade external interoperability are candidates for later phases unless discovery changes the scope.

\section{Requirements Principles}

Requirements must be supported by research, clinical workflow understanding, or explicit stakeholder decisions. A feature observed in another EMR is not automatically a requirement for this product.

\section{Functional Requirements --- Placeholder}

Detailed functional requirements will be added after the research and clinician interview. They will receive stable identifiers such as FR-PAT-001, FR-REC-001, and FR-APT-001 so that implementation and testing can be traced back to them.

\section{Non-Functional Requirements --- Placeholder}

The final specification will define measurable requirements for security, privacy, availability, performance, usability, maintainability, auditability, data integrity, and extensibility.

\section{Open Questions}

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \item Which patient fields are mandatory in the target clinical setting?
    \item Which clinical documentation must be structured versus free text?
    \item What is the exact signing/finalization workflow for medical reports?
    \item Who may mark a patient as deceased, and what information must be captured?
    \item What should happen to future appointments after deceased status is recorded?
    \item Which terminology and interoperability concepts should be represented in the MVP?
\end{itemize}

\section{Traceability}

The final SRS will link requirements to design decisions, implementation issues, tests, and release evidence. This is intended to make it possible to answer: \emph{why does this feature exist, where is it implemented, and how was it verified?}

\section{References}

References will be added as the research phase is completed. The research set includes established EMR platforms, WHO digital-health guidance, HL7 FHIR, and relevant terminology/interoperability standards.
