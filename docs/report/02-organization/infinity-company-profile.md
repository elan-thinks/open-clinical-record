# Infinity Advanced Technology Solutions PLC — Company Profile

> **Reporting period:** 01 September 2026 – 30 September 2026  
> **Primary internship location:** Addis Ababa, Ethiopia

## 1. Introduction

Infinity Advanced Technology Solutions PLC (IATS) is an Ethiopian organization operating at the intersection of healthcare technology, medical equipment, information technology, and technical services. Public information published by the organization describes Infinity as a healthcare-technology-oriented company whose activities extend beyond equipment supply into information technology, healthcare technology, service and support, and digital systems.

For this report, the company profile combines two evidence categories:

1. **Public organizational information**, used to describe the company and its publicly presented business activities.
2. **Firsthand internship observations**, used to describe the working environment encountered by the intern during September 2026.

This distinction is maintained because an internship report should not represent a limited period of personal observation as a complete description of an organization's entire operations.

## 2. Company Background

According to Infinity's public company profile, the organization was established in **2006** and was formerly known as **Boston Medical PLC**. The company's LinkedIn profile likewise describes Infinity as formerly known as Boston Medical PLC and identifies 2006 as its founding year. The organization presents itself as having offices or operations associated with Ethiopia and international locations, including the United States, Dubai, and Kenya.

Infinity's public profile describes its historical focus around medical equipment distribution, information technology, and technical services. Its stated business direction includes healthcare equipment, healthcare technology, IT solutions, and related professional services.

The company's public website describes its development as being driven by healthcare needs in Ethiopia and East Africa, with an emphasis on providing healthcare providers with equipment, technology, expertise, and ongoing support.

## 3. Healthcare and Medical-Technology Orientation

Healthcare is a central component of Infinity's public identity. Its website identifies services and solutions covering medical equipment, surgical solutions, laboratory solutions, medical consumables, IT, and service and support.

This orientation is particularly relevant to the OCR internship because the assigned project was an Electronic Medical Record application. The internship therefore took place in an organization whose public business profile includes both healthcare and information technology.

The connection is important from an industrial-learning perspective: the intern was not studying an abstract software domain in isolation. The assigned system belonged to a healthcare-technology context in which information management, clinical workflows, medical systems, and technology services intersect.

## 4. Information Technology Activities

Infinity's public website identifies Information Technology as one of its service areas. Its published IT portfolio includes infrastructure solutions, networking and security, servers and storage, data-center facilities, physical and data security, collaboration technologies, and cloud-related services.

The company also publicly describes enterprise software activities, including ERP implementation and customization.

Its ERP implementation material identifies **Oracle and Odoo** as technology partnerships and describes an implementation process involving:

- needs assessment;
- customization;
- integration;
- training and support;
- continuous optimization.

The process provides useful context for understanding the type of technology-service work associated with the organization, although the intern's personal experience should not be used to claim that every project follows exactly this process.

## 5. Odoo and Enterprise Systems

Infinity's public Odoo partner profile states that the organization initiated an Odoo partnership to address ERP needs and describes implementation across areas including CRM, finance, purchasing, projects, and services. The profile states that the organization has used Odoo for organizational automation since 2020.

During the internship, the intern also observed Odoo being used within the organizational environment. Therefore, Odoo exposure is both consistent with publicly available company information and supported by firsthand observation.

The Odoo experience was useful to the intern because it demonstrated that software systems used in an organization extend beyond the specific application being developed during an internship. Enterprise systems can connect administrative, operational, financial, project, and service processes.

## 6. Healthcare Information Systems

Infinity's public IT page describes **UniChart** as an Electronic Medical Record (EMR) system intended to centralize patient information and make it accessible to healthcare professionals. The company describes capabilities including patient-record management, integration with laboratory and imaging systems, customizable templates, security, and cloud-based access.

The same public material describes related healthcare information systems, including **UniRIS**, a radiology information system.

Infinity's ERP implementation page also identifies healthcare projects and describes work involving medical and information systems.

These public offerings provide important industrial context for the OCR internship. They demonstrate that healthcare information systems are part of the organization's publicly presented technology portfolio, while OCR was developed as a focused internship-scale EMR MVP.

## 7. PACS and RIS

Infinity's public service information identifies PACS (Picture Archiving and Communication System) and RIS (Radiological Information System) among its healthcare information technology services.

PACS is concerned with the storage, management, and communication of medical images, while RIS supports radiology-related workflows and information management.

The organization also publicly describes implementation and integration of healthcare information systems for medical organizations. These activities illustrate the broader ecosystem surrounding an EMR: patient information, clinical records, laboratory information, radiology information, imaging, and other healthcare workflows may need to interact in a mature healthcare information environment.

The OCR project deliberately did not attempt to reproduce this full ecosystem. Its internship scope was reduced to the core MVP areas of Patient Management, Patient Chart, and Appointment Management.

## 8. Publicly Described Clients and Industry Reach

Infinity's public materials identify work involving organizations in healthcare as well as other industries.

Its Odoo partner profile lists references in areas including manufacturing/maintenance, transportation/logistics, health/social welfare/pharmaceutical, food/hospitality/tourism/beverage, and construction/renovation.

Its public ERP material also identifies healthcare and non-healthcare applications, including Pioneer Diagnostic Center, Jimma University Medical Center, government and private hospitals, and construction and manufacturing organizations.

These references indicate that the organization's publicly described technology activities are not limited to one type of business process.

## 9. Organizational Scale

Infinity's public website states that the organization employs more than 140 people in Ethiopia. LinkedIn presents the company in the 51–200 employee size category.

Because these sources use different measurement and publication contexts, this report does not attempt to reconcile them into a single precise current headcount. The figures are presented only as publicly reported indicators of organizational scale.

## 10. Stated Vision and Mission

Infinity's public company profile states a vision centered on becoming a premier provider of medical equipment and supplies in Ethiopia and developing maintenance and service capabilities for the wider East African region.

Its published mission includes identifying, introducing, delivering, and installing relevant diagnostic medical equipment; developing long-term customer relationships through service; providing turnkey solutions; consulting customers; and continuously developing engineering capability.

These statements are presented here as **the organization's published statements**, not as an independent assessment of whether they have been achieved.

## 11. Governance and Ethics

Infinity's public website states that the company places importance on corporate governance and business ethics and refers to an employee code of conduct.

For this internship report, these statements are included as organizationally published information. The intern's direct observations during the one-month placement provide evidence about selected workplace practices, but do not constitute an independent audit of company-wide governance or ethics.

## 12. Workplace Exposure During the Internship

The intern's firsthand exposure occurred from **01 September 2026 to 30 September 2026**.

The first formal interaction with the organization was an **interview with HR representative Ms. Mahilet**. This initial interaction was significant because it preceded the technical internship work and introduced the organizational side of the placement.

During the internship, the intern observed a software-development environment in which team members worked on different tasks and, in some cases, from different locations in Ethiopia.

The intern observed team members:

- discussing progress and current tasks;
- communicating technical or work-related problems;
- identifying blockers;
- requesting resources or organizational support;
- receiving time and opportunity to learn technologies relevant to their work;
- participating in regular coordination meetings.

## 13. Weekly Wednesday Meetings

A recurring workplace activity observed during the internship was the Wednesday in-person meeting.

At approximately 3:00–4:00 local time, team members gathered in a meeting room for a session that generally lasted about one hour. Participants observed by the intern included HR, project management, other team members, and the intern.

The meeting provided an example of organizational coordination in practice. Team members discussed their work, progress, problems, and needs rather than focusing exclusively on source-code implementation.

This exposure helped the intern understand that industrial software development is simultaneously a technical and organizational activity.

## 14. Relevance to the OCR Internship

The organization's combination of healthcare and IT activities made the environment particularly relevant to an EMR project.

The intern's OCR assignment could therefore be understood within a broader technology landscape involving:

- healthcare delivery;
- medical equipment;
- healthcare information systems;
- enterprise software;
- ERP;
- radiology and imaging systems;
- IT infrastructure;
- technical service and support.

At the same time, OCR remained an internship-scale system. The mentor explicitly directed the intern to reduce the initial scope and focus on Patient Management, Patient Chart, and Appointment Management rather than attempting to reproduce a full enterprise healthcare platform.

## 15. Industrial Significance

The company context influenced the intern's understanding of the project in several ways.

First, the healthcare setting made it clear that software requirements are strongly influenced by real-world workflows. A patient record is not merely a database row; it is part of a longitudinal clinical process.

Second, exposure to enterprise systems such as Odoo demonstrated that organizational software often contains many interconnected processes and user roles.

Third, the organization's broader healthcare-technology portfolio provided context for understanding why mature healthcare systems may involve EMR, laboratory, radiology, imaging, infrastructure, and integration concerns.

Finally, the internship demonstrated that the scale of an industrial software environment can be much larger than the scope of a student MVP. Good engineering therefore requires deliberate scope management.
