# Organization Profile — Evidence Record

## 1. Organization

**Infinity Advanced Technology Solutions PLC**

**Location:** Addis Ababa, Ethiopia

The internship was completed in an environment that included software development activities, project management, human-resources coordination, and regular team communication.

## 2. Evidence Boundary

This report distinguishes between information directly observed by the intern and organizational facts that require independent verification.

### Directly observed / firsthand

During the internship, the intern observed:

- a development team working on different software tasks;
- team members working from different locations in Ethiopia;
- developers discussing current tasks and technical/work-related problems;
- developers communicating resource and support needs;
- organizational support for learning technologies relevant to assigned work;
- regular Wednesday in-person meetings;
- participation in those meetings by HR, project management, developers/team members, and the intern;
- individual progress and issue discussions during the meetings.

### Project-specific evidence

The OCR internship project was a simplified EMR application developed as an internship assignment. The project repository contains the requirements, research, architecture, implementation, testing, and finalization evidence.

### Information requiring verification before final publication

The following should not be presented as established company facts until supported by an official source or company-provided material:

- company founding date;
- legal/company history;
- official vision and mission;
- complete service portfolio;
- organizational chart;
- exact number of employees;
- complete technology portfolio;
- formal department structure;
- client/customer information;
- claims about company-wide development practices beyond what the intern observed.

## 3. Workplace Environment

The internship provided exposure to an active software-development environment rather than an isolated student project.

The intern observed communication between developers and organizational support roles concerning work progress, problems, resource requirements, and learning needs. This demonstrated that industrial software development includes coordination and support activities in addition to implementation.

## 4. Weekly In-Person Meetings

Every Wednesday, team members met in person in a meeting room at approximately 3–4 LT. Meetings generally lasted around one hour.

Participants observed by the intern included:

- Ms. Mahilet — HR representative;
- Sir Ostin — Project Manager;
- other team members present;
- the intern.

The meetings followed an individual discussion pattern in which team members discussed their current work, progress, problems, and resource/support needs.

## 5. Organizational Learning

The internship gave the intern practical exposure to several aspects of software work that are not represented by source code alone:

- progress communication;
- problem/blocker reporting;
- requesting resources;
- project coordination;
- management/developer communication;
- continuous technical learning;
- working with distributed team members.

## 6. OCR Internship Placement

The intern's principal technical assignment was Open Clinical Record (OCR), a focused outpatient EMR MVP.

The project was developed within a time-boxed internship context and was deliberately scoped toward three core areas:

1. Patient Management
2. Patient Chart
3. Appointment Management

The repository documents the resulting system and its engineering process.

## 7. Odoo Exposure

The intern also observed the organization's use of Odoo for organizational/operational activities. This should be described as workplace exposure unless the intern's direct interaction with Odoo can be documented more specifically.

## 8. Reporting Principle

The organization chapter should describe what the intern actually encountered without turning limited workplace exposure into unsupported claims about the entire company. This distinction is especially important for a formal academic report.
