# Internship Placement and Workplace Role

## 1. Internship Placement

The internship placed the intern within an active software-development environment at Infinity Advanced Technology Solutions PLC in Addis Ababa.

The principal technical assignment was the development of Open Clinical Record (OCR), a simplified Electronic Medical Record application.

## 2. Nature of the Assignment

The assignment was designed to expose the intern to the software development lifecycle rather than to a single programming task. The work covered:

- domain and EMR research;
- requirements analysis;
- scope definition;
- system and data design;
- user-interface planning;
- application development;
- authentication and authorization;
- testing;
- documentation;
- final presentation and demonstration.

## 3. Role of the Mentor

The mentor provided guidance throughout the project and helped the intern distinguish between an unnecessarily broad EMR scope and a realistic internship MVP.

A particularly important intervention was the reduction of the original scope to Patient Management, Patient Chart, and Appointment Management. This allowed the intern to concentrate on completing coherent core workflows within the available period.

## 4. Role of the Project Environment

The broader workplace environment provided exposure to:

- project-management communication;
- developer coordination;
- resource requests;
- technical problem discussion;
- learning and upskilling support;
- regular progress review.

The Wednesday meetings were especially useful for observing these processes.

## 5. Relationship Between Academic Learning and Industrial Practice

The internship provided an opportunity to apply software-engineering concepts in a real project context. Concepts such as requirements engineering, software architecture, database modeling, authentication, authorization, testing, version control, and technical documentation became connected to a single working system.

The experience also revealed aspects that are harder to learn through coursework alone, particularly scope negotiation, documentation-driven development, communication of blockers, and the relationship between technical work and organizational support.
