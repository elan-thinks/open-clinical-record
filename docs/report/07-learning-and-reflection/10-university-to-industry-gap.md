# 7.10 University-to-Industry Gap

## Connected systems

University courses provide important foundations, but the internship showed that industrial engineering requires those concepts to be connected in one system.

For OCR, object-oriented design, databases, software engineering, web development, testing, security, and project management interacted continuously.

## Requirements engineering

Academic assignments can make requirements appear stable. The internship demonstrated that requirements are constrained by time, stakeholder needs, capacity, and implementation reality.

The mentor's scope reduction was a concrete example: the possible feature list was larger than the practical one-month deliverable.

## Architecture and quality

The internship showed why maintainable boundaries matter when requirements, testing, security, and documentation interact. Quality also became broader than "the application runs"; a professional system should be testable, explainable, reproducible, and reviewable.

## Documentation and domain knowledge

Industrial documentation should allow another person to understand what the system does, why decisions were made, how it should be tested, and where limitations remain.

Healthcare software also demonstrated that technical skills alone do not determine whether a workflow is sensible. Domain concepts must be understood before behavior is designed.

## Reflection

The main gap was not the absence of individual technical concepts. It was integrating them under real constraints and making decisions whose consequences cross multiple system parts.
