# 7.9 Mentor and Team Learning

## Industrial team exposure

The internship provided exposure to a development environment in which work was coordinated across people working from different locations. The intern observed discussions about blockers, resources, progress, and time needed to learn unfamiliar technologies.

## Mentor guidance

The mentor provided the project roadmap, reviewed progress, allowed technology choices, introduced existing EMR concepts, and helped constrain scope.

The scope reduction was particularly educational: the project was narrowed to Patient Management, Patient Chart, and Appointment Management.

## Weekly meetings

Weekly Wednesday meetings provided regular checkpoints for progress and issues. This showed that professional work is not judged only at the final presentation; progress and blockers are discussed during development.

## Communicating blockers

A practical lesson was that blockers should be communicated rather than hidden until a deadline. Technical uncertainty, environment issues, and scope constraints made progress communication part of engineering work.

Professional communication is part of technical delivery. A technically correct implementation still needs clear scope, status, risks, and evidence.
